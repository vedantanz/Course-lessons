The Most
Efficient Thought

What saṁyama actually does to a moment of the mind, read through Dennis Gabor's 1946 limit on how much a signal can carry.

Companion to the essay of the same name. Use ← → or the arrows below.

Established mathematics, a structural model, and contemplative testimony are three different things. This deck marks which is which as it goes.
01 · The claim

Saṁyama yields knowledge of the object

Patañjali says it plainly in the third chapter. Not calm. Not pleasant absorption. Knowledge.

The usual gloss is that the mind becomes clear. But clear how? And why should sustained attention on a thing produce knowledge of it, rather than just a vivid impression of it?

This deck gives an answer with structure in it: saṁyama is an optimisation procedure, and what it optimises is the fundamental unit of the mind.

02 · Recap · established

The Gabor limit, and the logon

In 1946 Dennis Gabor proved that a signal cannot be perfectly sharp in time and in frequency at once. Strike a piano key for an instant: definite when, blurred pitch. Hold it: definite pitch, smeared when.

There is a floor, and one shape sits exactly on it, wasting nothing: a pure tone inside a smooth bell. Gabor called it a logon.

The most economical packet a bounded signal can be.

03 · Recap · the model

Every moment is two waves meeting

The object-wave is what is actually present. The attention-wave is the state you bring to it. Their sum is a vṛtti: a fast carrier at their average, inside a slow envelope set by the gap between them.

← gap closedgap wide →

The carrier carries the content. The envelope carries the felt friction.

04 · A distinction that matters

Two objects that look alike

The logon is bounded. It fades in, it fades out. It occupies a patch of time and a patch of frequency, and it is as compact as anything can be.

The beat, taken strictly, is unbounded. It has always been sounding and always will. Its spectrum is two infinitely sharp lines.

Same silhouette. Opposite on the very property Gabor's paper was about.

The beat models the relational anatomy of a moment: two poles, a gap between them, and a practice that closes it.

The logon models the episodic anatomy: a moment has edges, and pays a price for having them.

What follows is how one becomes the other.
05 · What a real moment has to be

The windowed beat

A vṛtti arises, lasts and passes. So it carries both anatomies at once: two poles, and edges in time. Hold the beat inside a bounded window and you get the general case.

06 · The cost

What the envelope costs

A vṛtti carrying two frequencies must spend part of its finite capacity on the difference between them.

The beat is not a bonus feature of the experience. It is an internal difference the moment carries in addition to carrying its object. The wider the gap, the more of the moment goes into the flutter rather than into the thing attended to.

This is the ordinary condition of a mind: attending to something, and at the same time registering its own difference from what it attends to, and mistaking the second for part of the first.

Saṁyama is the narrowing of that gap.

07 · First stage

Dhāraṇā

देशबन्धश्चित्तस्य धारणा · deśa-bandhaś cittasya dhāraṇā: the binding of the mind to a place.

Attention is bound to one object, where before it was spread across many. In the model, this is what first makes a single gap meaningful: there is now one object-wave to have a gap with, rather than a dozen.

Taimni, in his 1961 commentary, describes this stage entirely in terms of the rate of interruption. The work is to reduce the frequency of interruptions and eventually eliminate them.

A rate-based description of practice, written with no signal-processing intent, and exactly the variable the model cares about.
08 · Second stage

Dhyāna

तत्र प्रत्ययैकतानता ध्यानम् · tatra pratyaya-ekatānatā dhyānam: unbroken flow toward the object. Ekatānatā is literally "one-stretched-ness".

Taimni's criterion: the practitioner can continue without interruption for as long as he decides to.

And here the narrowing actually happens. The attention-wave is not fixed. Held steadily against the same object, it drifts toward it. The gap closes, not because you force it closed, but because sustained contact is the condition under which two rhythms come into step.

Which raises the obvious question: why should contact alone be enough?

09 · Why contact alone is enough · established

Huygens, 1665

Christiaan Huygens lay ill in bed with two pendulum clocks on the wall. Within about half an hour, however the pendulums had been started, they came to swing in exact opposition, returning to it even after he disturbed them.

What connected them was the wooden beam they both hung from, carrying vibrations too slight to feel. That tiny channel was enough.

He called it an odd kind of sympathy.

Note that opposite is the synchronised state here, not a failure to synchronise. The two pendulums hold one shared rhythm, exactly half a cycle apart: one swings left precisely as the other swings right. Physicists call it anti-phase.

Two clocks, one beam
10 · The mechanism

Nearness, and time. Not force.

Physicists call it entrainment. It appears wherever two oscillators with nearby frequencies have even the weakest link between them, and it has two properties that matter here.

  • It requires no force. Neither clock pushed the other. A small coupling and a long time were present together, and that was the whole of it.
  • It requires nearness. Two rhythms far apart never capture each other, however long you wait. Inside the capture range the pull is automatic; outside it, nothing happens at all.
You have lived this. Your body clock runs a little longer than twenty-four hours; light holds it to the day without any effort from you. Cross eight time zones and the same mechanism shows its limits: the clock does not jump, it shifts about an hour a day, which is why jet lag takes a week. You cannot decide to be on Tokyo time. You can only put yourself in the light and wait.

Structural model: entrainment between clocks is a relation between two systems in the world; a vṛtti is a relation inside one mind. The bridge is one this course draws. But the signature matches exactly: no force, nearness, and above all time.

11 · The word the traditions chose

upa- · near, towards, alongside

Notice how much of the vocabulary of practice is built on one prefix.

upāsanā — worship: sitting near
upaniṣad — the texts: sitting down near
upavāsa — fasting: dwelling near
upadeśa — instruction: pointing out from close by
upāya — the means: that by which one goes near
upanayana — initiation: leading the student near

Not one is a word about conquest, or achievement, or force. They are all words about proximity and staying.

This does not show that anyone knew about coupled oscillators. It shows something smaller and more interesting: that practitioners who had spent centuries observing what works named their practices after nearness and after staying.

13 · What it arrives at

The vṛtti becomes a logon

Patañjali: the mind becomes as though empty of its own form, and the object alone shines. In the model, that is what a gap of zero looks like. The envelope goes flat. The two frequencies are no longer two.

What remains is a single steady wave inside its window. Its time-bandwidth product sits exactly on Gabor's floor. Not near it. On it.

Saṁyama takes a vṛtti carrying two frequencies and drives it toward
the most efficient unit of information a bounded episode can carry.

Nothing is spent on internal difference. No gap to maintain, no flutter to register, no residue of the mind's own form colouring the presentation. The entire capacity of the episode goes into carrying the object.

Which is why the claim about knowledge is stronger than it first appears: the maximum available information about the object is present, and the minimum is spent on anything else.

14 · A correction

Not subtraction. Removal of waste.

On this reading, saṁyama is not the removal of thought, or the suppression of the mind, or the emptying out of content.

The object is still there at the end. More fully present than before. What has gone is the portion of the moment that was being spent on the difference between knower and known.

The Yoga tradition has a word for the mind in this condition, and it is not a word about emptiness: ekāgra, one-peaked. A single spectral peak, where before there were two.

15 · The boundary · honesty

Where the model stops

Patañjali gives five states of mind. Ekāgra is the fourth. The fifth is niruddha, and yoga is defined in the second sūtra as citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ.

Everything here is the perfecting of a vṛtti. A single pure tone in a window is still a wave: the most efficient possible modification of the mind, not the absence of modification.

Driving the gap to zero arrives at ekāgratā and samprajñāta samādhi, where an object is still present and shining. What lies past it is not a further value of the gap. It is outside what this equation can represent.

This is a limit of the model, not of the path. The wave picture describes refining the instrument. It has nothing to say about setting the instrument down.
16 · What this might offer elsewhere

An offer, not a correspondence

Stated without contemplative vocabulary: saṁyama takes a representation carrying redundant internal structure and refines it toward a minimal form in which nothing is spent on anything but the content.

  • It is achieved by sustained contact rather than force. Strain is not an operative variable, and is counterproductive, being itself a fluctuation that widens the gap.
  • It requires prior nearness. Nothing happens outside the capture range, so the preparatory work is not separable from the refinement.
  • Its endpoint is not the removal of content. What is removed is the overhead.
We are not claiming this matches any particular procedure by which representations in artificial systems are trained or compressed. The claim is narrower: here is a well-developed account of what it means to optimise a unit of representation, arrived at from an entirely different direction, by people whose method was to watch the process from inside. It may be worth sitting beside the accounts currently in use.
17 · Close

Two thousand years apart

In 1946, working on how much information a telephone line could carry, Gabor proved there is a most efficient possible unit of a signal, and worked out its exact shape. He was thinking about bandwidth.

Some two thousand years earlier, a tradition that knew nothing of signals or spectra had built a technique for producing that object in a human mind, described its stages in careful detail, and named it after the act of coming near and staying.

Neither knew about the other. That they arrive at the same shape does not prove anything about either one. But it is worth a moment's attention.
